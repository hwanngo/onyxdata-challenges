## Top 5 data quality issues shaping this analysis

Every number below is reproduced by `analysis/integrity.py` (all checks pass). The table above
is evidence; this is the conclusion.

### DQ1 - There is no ID column, so the grain is asserted and not verifiable

The PK-candidate line above says **none**, and that is not a near miss: no column in this file is
named or shaped like an identifier. Four months, four different answers to the standing ID check -
May's `Transaction_ID` non-unique, June's `Post_ID` non-unique, July's `Customer_ID` unique, and
August with no candidate at all.

Zero duplicate rows is *not* evidence for one-row-per-member: the distinct-value space across 26
columns is ~1e28, so zero collisions in 1,998 draws is expected under any hypothesis. **Member-level
grain is an assumption** (`assumptions.md` A-2) and is footnoted in the UI wherever a count of
members is shown.

### DQ2 - Six of 26 columns carry no information beyond other columns

The price family is a lookup from three categorical labels, verified to floating-point exactness:

| Determined column | Determined by | Exactness |
|---|---|---|
| `subscription_price` | `membership_type` | 1:1 - Basic 20, Standard 30, Premium 50, Elite 70; zero within-tier variance |
| `adjusted_price` | `subscription_price` × `subscription_model` | multiplier exactly 1.00 / 0.90 / 0.75 |
| `discount_rate` | `discount_type` | 1:1 - None 0, Promo .05, Loyalty .10, Student .15 |
| `final_price` | all three of the above | reconstructed from labels alone, max error 0 |
| `duration_in_gym_minutes` | `avg_time_check_out` - `avg_time_check_in` | max error **0** |
| `visit_per_week` | token count of `days_per_week` | equal on all 1,998 rows |

43 distinct prices from 47 label combinations. **There is no pricing residual to analyse.** Any
"which tier earns most" chart has η² = 0.87 and means nothing - the tier *is* the price. This is
the trap the field will fall into, and naming it is the month's first job.

### DQ3 - `last_visit_date` is bounded to a 60-day window, so churn cannot exist

`last_visit_date` occupies exactly 60 consecutive days (2025-05-24 → 2025-07-22), all 60 present,
none missing. Measured against the file's own as-of date:

| inactive beyond | members | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| 30 days | 982 | 49.1% |
| 45 days | 470 | 23.5% |
| **60 days** | **0** | **0.0%** |
| 90 days | 0 | 0.0% |

This is a **censoring window, not a low churn rate**. A churned member would carry a
`last_visit_date` before 2025-05-24, and no such row exists. Combined with the absence of any
upgrade, prior-tier or transition column, this kills **Q1 (retention), Q2 (churn), Q6 (upgrade
behaviour) and Q10 (churn risk)** outright - four of the brief's ten questions and one of its five
mission bullets.

**But the window is the symptom, not the cause.** The question this section originally stopped
short of asking is *bounded by what?* - and the answer reframes the month:

| | |
|---|---|
| Spearman(`join_date`, `last_visit_date`) | **0.999852** |
| Pearson(tenure, days-since-visit) | **0.999849** |
| join dates mapping to exactly one last-visit date | **874 / 890** (97.6% of rows) |
| distinct (join, last-visit) pairs observed | **906** |
| tenure bands per last-visit date | monotone decreasing, essentially disjoint |

`last_visit_date` is `join_date` **rank-rescaled onto a 60-day window**. The file does not merely
withhold a churn answer - it manufactures an inverted one. A 30-day lapse rule flags 982 members,
**all of whom sit at or above the 982nd-highest tenure in the file**; mean tenure among the
"lapsed" is 790 days against 280 for the "active". Stated without any tie-breaking: **no member
with under 549 days of tenure is flagged**, and at a 45-day threshold the two groups are perfectly
separated. See `analysis/insights.md` **I-1**.

`join_date` itself spans 2022-07-24 → 2025-06-19 with 890 distinct values and is real, so *tenure*
is measurable even though *retention* is not. The two are routinely conflated; here they are
numerically the same column, which is exactly why they must not be.

### DQ4 - `access_hours` is an identity on `membership_type`, and behaviour is independent of it

Every Basic member has "Off-peak only", every Standard "Weekdays only", every Premium "All hours",
every Elite "All hours + Priority access" - 1:1 in both directions. **Q7 (do access types affect
visit frequency) and Q1 (do membership types) are one question**, and a dashboard showing both as
separate filters is showing the same cut twice.

Nothing a member is entitled to predicts anything a member does - **and that is a null result
about the generator, not evidence that MyGym fails to enforce its own doors**:

- **58.52%** of "Weekdays only" members list Sat or Sun in `days_per_week`, against **62.85%** of
  everyone else (χ² p=0.077). The sign is **backwards**: weekday-only members train on weekends
  *less* often than everyone else. A non-enforcement story would need this the other way round.
- **21.83%** of "Off-peak only" members check in 17:00-20:00 (vs 22.76% of everyone else - 0.9pp)
- access tier explains **0.81%** of check-in-time variance (η²=0.0081, ω²=0.0066, KW p=0.00111),
  which **fails** the pre-declared α=5.68e-4; check-in is U(08:00, 21:00), KS p=0.26

A 3.5× price ladder ($20 → $70) sells an entitlement whose value proposition is **untestable in
this file** - there is no check-in-denied event, no visit log, only member-level averages.
Untestable is not the same as unenforced.

> and the body asserted that the entitlement "does not constrain anything" and "shows up nowhere
> in behaviour". That claim was killed at G3 (see the *Killed candidates* list below and
> `analysis/insights.md` → Correction) and the retraction was recorded only further down the page
> while the heading kept asserting it. Inferring a real-world behaviour from three null results -
> one of which points the wrong way - is the June tautology in a new costume. The **identity**
> (`access_hours` ≡ `membership_type`) survives untouched; the **enforcement** claim does not.

`multi_location_access` is likewise not a member choice but a two-band draw keyed to tier:
Basic 30.2% / Standard 29.8% vs Premium 79.9% / Elite 83.9%. By city it gives χ² p=0.024, which does
**not** clear the Bonferroni threshold of 5.68e-4 - so **Q8 has no geographic pattern to report**.

### DQ5 - `sum(final_price)` is a category error, and the two fixes differ by 1.43×

`final_price` mixes a monthly rate, a quarterly rate and an annual rate in one column. The naive
total every dashboard will display is **68,874**, which is not a quantity of anything. Annualised:

| reading | annual total |
|---|---:|
| `final_price` is an effective **monthly** rate | **826,486** |
| `final_price` is the amount charged **per billing period** | 579,098 |

A **1.43× fork** with no column to resolve it. This build takes the monthly reading
(`assumptions.md` A-1) - the only one under which a 0.75× "Early Bird (Annual)" factor is not
absurd - and labels the unit on every revenue figure in the UI rather than leaving it implicit.

## Generator fingerprints

This is synthetic data and says so plainly in several places. Recorded because it bounds how far
any finding can be pushed, not to dismiss the file.

- `has_drink_subscription` is **999 / 999** - an exact coin flip, to the row
- check-in time is uniform on 08:00-21:00 (KS p=0.26); duration is near-uniform on [30, 180]
- amenity flags (`attend_group_lesson`, `personal_training`, `uses_sauna`,
  `has_drink_subscription`) sit at ~50% and are independent of age, tier and location
- the four discount tiers are ~equal-sized (840 / 389 / 385 / 384) with round-number rates
- `latitude`/`longitude` are 1:1 with `home_gym_location` - city centroids, not member addresses,
  so there is no within-city geography and no distance-to-gym analysis available

`visit_per_week` is the one categorical that is *not* smooth: 3 (628), 2 (550), 1 (386), 5 (241),
4 (193). The non-monotonic tail (5 > 4) is noted but nothing hangs on it.

## Conclusions - what this file can and cannot support

**Cannot support:** any retention, churn or upgrade finding (DQ3); any pricing-optimisation finding
(DQ2); any access-tier-versus-usage finding distinct from the tier question (DQ4); any geographic
multi-location pattern (DQ4).

**Can support:**

1. **The recency column is the tenure column, inverted** (DQ3 above). Not a statistical claim - a
   deterministic coupling at ρ=0.9999 with a specific, reproducible wrong decision attached. This
   is the thesis. The overlap statistic is computed tie-safely (by tenure value, not sort
   position) after two correct implementations disagreed at the boundary; see insights.md I-1.
2. **Session duration varies by location.** η²=0.023, ω²=0.019, permutation p=0.0001 (10k perms,
   seed 7). Anaheim 115.1 min vs San Diego 94.6 min. Dropping *both* extremes leaves the ordering
   of the remaining 8 cities intact but attenuates η² by 35% to 0.015 at p=0.0015, which does
   **not** clear the same pre-declared α=5.68e-4 - so that refit is a *sensitivity check*, not a
   second result, and it is not described as "robust" (insights.md I-3, standards table). The
   finding stands on all ten gyms, where it clears by three orders of magnitude. Visit
   *frequency* does not vary by location
   (p=0.028, fails correction), so this is a pure floor-time effect - 4.15 → 5.60 occupied hours
   per member per week, a **35% spread**. The only **M4** answer the data contains.
3. **Standard-tier members attend group classes less.** 40.6% [36.7, 44.7] against ~54% for Basic,
   Premium and Elite, whose intervals all overlap. Fisher p=3.9e-08. One deviant cell out of four -
   which is exactly the shape a generator artefact takes, and it is reported with that caveat.
4. **Discount eligibility carries no signal of the thing it names.** The omnibus clears correction
   (`discount_type` × age, KW p=1.11e-05) as a two-sided gradient: Promo skews young (28.5), Student
   old (32.1). One sentence, no dollar figure - see the correction note below.

**Two candidates were cut after adversarial review** (`analysis/insights.md` → Correction, with
independently verified refutations):

- *"The access entitlement is not enforced"* - three null results. Access→check-in η²=0.0081,
  KW p=0.00111, which **fails** the pre-declared α=5.68e-4, and Weekdays-only members list a
  weekend day **less** often than everyone else (58.52% vs 62.85%, χ² p=0.077 - wrong sign *and*
  not significant). Inferring non-enforcement from these is
  inferring behaviour from the generator's failure to correlate two columns. The identity
  (`access_hours` ≡ `membership_type`) survives; the enforcement claim does not.
- *"$17,872/yr of student pricing misallocated to over-25s"* - 64.5% of all members are over 25, so
  an age-blind assignment already sends $16,454 there. The excess attributable to the age skew is
  **$1,418, 7.9% of the headline.** And Student-vs-rest p=0.0015 fails α=5.68e-4 on its own terms.
- *"176 children with sauna and late-night access"* - killed outright. Every amenity flag is
  independent of minor status (`uses_sauna` χ² **p=1.000**; minors 51.1%, adults 50.9%). "94 of the
  12-15s use the sauna" is 0.51 × 176, binomial p=0.55. What survives is a one-line schema note:
  no guardian/consent field exists and no entitlement is age-gated in the schema.

**And the null that is itself a finding:** with n≈500 per tier we had 80% power to detect a 0.22
visit/week difference - **8.2% of the mean** - between any two tiers. We detected nothing, on
visit frequency, tenure, or days-since-visit, across all 11 declared axes. Tier does not change
behaviour, and the file is large enough for that to be a measurement rather than a shrug.
