# Conclusions

## Grain

**One row per post: 5,600 posts over 487 days (2024-01-01 → 2025-05-01), ~11.5 posts/day.** Unlike
May, this is a genuine event table, not a daily summary - the analysis has real statistical power.

Our totals reconcile with the field's strongest entry (impressions 5,766,555,744 ≈ its 5,766.56M;
engagement 646,491,442 ≈ its 646.49M), so the read path is sound.

## Top 5 data quality issues shaping this analysis

**DQ1 · `Post_ID` is not a key.** 5,000 distinct values across 5,600 rows; 557 IDs are reused, one
4×. The rows they label are genuinely distinct (0 duplicates ignoring the ID), so this is a broken
identifier rather than duplicated data - but nothing may be counted by it. **This is the same defect
as May's `Transaction_ID`, in a different dataset: a generator habit, not a coincidence.** Assume
every future month's "ID" column is guilty until tested.

**DQ2 · `Engagement` is not the sum of its components.** `Engagement == Likes + Shares + Comments`
holds on **0 of 5,600** rows; the ratio averages 0.58 and ranges 0.15-1.49. A social report would
naturally present engagement as likes + shares + comments, and here that is simply false.
`Engagement` is an independent measure. Never decompose it.

**DQ3 · Click-through is structurally absent for half the platforms.** `Clicks` and
`Click_Through_Rate` are **66.8% null**, and the missingness is almost entirely structural - a
platform property, not a random draw:

| Platform | posts with CTR |
|---|---|
| TikTok | 1,257 / 1,257 - **100%** |
| Facebook | 299 / 299 - **100%** |
| LinkedIn | 304 / 525 - 57.9% |
| YouTube · X.com · Instagram | **0** |

Within LinkedIn it is decided mostly, but not entirely, by post type:

| LinkedIn post type | posts with CTR | |
|---|---|---|
| Article | 207 / 207 - **100%** | always |
| Live Stream | 68 / 68 - **100%** | always |
| Carousel | 25 / 44 - 56.8% | **partial** |
| PDF | 4 / 16 - 25.0% | **partial** |
| Image · Text · Video | 0 / 190 | never |

> **Correction (2025-06-30).** This section previously stopped at "Article and Live Stream always;
> Image, Text and Video never" and concluded **"CTR exists exactly where a post can carry a link"**
> - deterministic missingness. That is false, and the table above shows why: 207 + 68 = 275, but
> LinkedIn has **304** CTR rows. The 29 that were unaccounted for are Carousel and PDF, the two
> cells that are neither all nor nothing. The rule as it survives its own data: click tracking is a
> *platform* property (TikTok and Facebook every post; YouTube, X.com and Instagram none) with
> LinkedIn as the exception, and inside LinkedIn it is decided by post type for five of seven types
> and unexplained for the other two. Nothing we tested separates the 29 from the 31 - not content
> type, region, hour, date, engagement or any view/impression/like/share/comment measure. See
> `analysis/insights.md` I-5. **Do not impute the blanks.**

Structural absence is coherent business logic rather than a defect - but it means requirements
**R5** (regional click-through) and **R6** (hashtags driving clicks) are answerable for only
**33.2%** of posts, and any platform ranking on CTR compares three platforms while silently
dropping three.

**DQ4 · `Engagement_Level` disagrees with its own thresholds.** The labels are bands of
`Engagement_Rate` (Low < 0.10, Medium < 0.20, High ≥ 0.20), but **89 rows (1.6%)** sit below 0.10
and are labelled Medium. Small, but it means the label and the rate cannot both be trusted; we use
the rate and derive the band ourselves.

**DQ5 · The brief misdescribes the dataset.** It names four platforms (the data has **six** -
Facebook and YouTube are omitted, and YouTube is the *largest* at 1,320 posts) and calls it "a 2024
dataset" (it runs 17 months, to 2025-05-01). Recorded in `brief.md` and `assumptions.md`; the report
uses what the data contains and footnotes the discrepancy.

## Also true, and useful

- **`Engagement_Rate` is exactly `Engagement / Views`** (max deviation 9.6e-04, i.e. stored rounded
  to ~4dp). `Click_Through_Rate` is exactly `Clicks / Impressions` (r = 1.0000). Both are honest
  derived measures - no fan-out, no broken arithmetic.
- **The pre-registered noise test was misleading, and this is the lesson of the month.**
  `Engagement_Rate` is not uniform *overall* (KS p = 2.1e-38), which I first read as "the metric
  carries real signal". It fails the global test **because it is a mixture of four uniform bands** -
  testing the pooled distribution hides exactly the structure that matters. **Test for uniformity
  within groups, not just globally.** Had I stopped at the global test, the tautology would have
  reached the poster.
- **Publishing hour is bounded to 08:00-19:00** and its row counts are bimodal - 10-12 and 15-17
  hold ~750 posts each while 08, 09, 13, 14, 18, 19 hold ~170-210. That shape is a generator
  artifact, so hour *volume* means nothing.
- **Region is eight countries** (USA, Canada, UK, Brazil, India, Australia, Japan, Germany), each
  with a single lat/long centroid - so it is a country dimension labelled "Region", and any map is
  eight points, not a choropleth.

## ⚠ CORRECTION - the first version of this section was tautological

An adversarial re-review (and independent re-verification, `integrity.py`) demolished the reading
below before it reached the ledger. **`Engagement_Rate` is not a measurement, it is a label.** Every
post's rate was drawn from a fixed uniform band keyed to its content tier:

| Band | Hashtag family | n | inside band | KS vs uniform |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| U(0.05, 0.10) | #FunContent, #MemeMonday, #JustForFun | 316 | 100% | p=0.155 |
| U(0.08, 0.15) | #ProductDemo, #FeatureHighlight, #SaaSLaunch, #NewRelease | 1,378 | 100% | p=0.481 |
| U(0.10, 0.18) | #EventRecap, #WebinarReplay, #EventSummary, #BehindTheScenes | 577 | 100% | p=0.425 |
| U(0.15, 0.25) | #SuccessStory, #CustomerStory, #Testimonial, #CustomerSuccess | 2,669 | 100% | p=0.043 |

88.2% of rows map cleanly; the only three hashtags that don't are the ones straddling categories.
Round-number bounds, uniform within band, 100% containment.

**So "content category explains 79% of engagement-rate variance" is arithmetically true and
behaviourally empty.** It restates the generator's parameter table. Educational does not out-engage
Entertainment 2.6× because audiences prefer it - the file was built that way. The numbers in the
table below are all correct and all reproduce; it is the *interpretation* that was wrong, and it is
the finding every published entry is implicitly reporting without noticing.

**Two more derived columns, same problem:**

- **`Impressions` = `Views` × U(1.1, 1.3)** - ratio support exactly [1.100009, 1.299975],
  KS p=0.684, r=0.9979. Impressions carries **zero** information beyond Views. Never chart one
  against the other; never present impressions as a separate reach metric.
- **`Engagement` = `Engagement_Rate` × `Views`** - median relative deviation 6.3e-06. Fully
  determined.

**Which leaves exactly one free variable: `Views`.** It is genuinely distributed - not uniform
within any post type or category (KS p=0.000 throughout, ranges overlapping 1,000-4.4M), not
lognormal (p=3.3e-49). Everything else in the file is derived from it, drawn from a label, or
structurally absent.

**That is the honest thesis for G3:** the only real question this dataset can answer is *what drives
views*, and the answer is **format, not placement** - `Post_Type` explains 19.0% of log-views
(Video median 913,870 vs Image 272,708, a 3.4× spread) while `Platform` explains 0.4% (p=0.048) and
`Region` 0.2% (p=0.30, not significant). The brief asks about platforms and regions. Neither moves
anything.

Also corrected: the 89 `Engagement_Level` mislabels are **not** undifferentiated - all 89 are on
Instagram (54) and LinkedIn (35), zero on the other four platforms. Instagram is 17.8% of the file
and 60.7% of the mislabels.

*Retained below because the numbers are correct and the reframing depends on them.*

## The main event - what actually explains engagement

Variance in `Engagement_Rate` explained by each axis (η², with a Kruskal-Wallis p):

| Axis | p | η² | share of variance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **`Content_Category`** | 0.0000 | **0.7936** | **79.4%** |
| `Main_Hashtag` | 0.0000 | 0.7505 | 75.1% - *but see below* |
| `Post_Type` | 0.0000 | 0.0556 | 5.6% |
| `Platform` | 0.0001 | 0.0049 | **0.5%** |
| `Region` | 0.0700 | 0.0023 | 0.2% |
| `Post_Hour` | 0.1914 | - | not significant |
| `Content_Type` (organic/sponsored) | 0.4129 | 0.0001 | not significant |

> **This table is about the engagement rate only, and the rate is a label (DQ1).** Two of these
> rows behave differently on `Views`, the one real column, and the report must not carry a
> rate-result over to views: `Content_Type` is *significant* on views (median 471,713 sponsored
> against 365,325 organic, Kruskal p=1.8e-06) and the effect is format mix that reverses inside
> Video - see `analysis/insights.md` I-8. `Post_Hour` and `Day_Name` are non-significant on both
> (I-7). Everything the dashboard publishes is tested on views.

**Every axis the brief points at is negligible, and the one it treats as metadata is nearly the
whole story.** The objective asks what makes content succeed *on different platforms* and for
*regional trends* - platform explains 0.5% of the variance and region 0.2%. Content category
explains 79%:

| Content category | mean engagement rate | n |
|---|---:|---:|
| Educational | **0.1989** | 2,026 |
| Customer Story | 0.1988 | 735 |
| Event / Webinar | 0.1394 | 577 |
| Product Promotion | 0.1159 | 1,385 |
| Entertainment | **0.0751** | 877 |

A **2.6×** spread from top to bottom, on near-disjoint ranges.

**The hashtag mirage.** `Main_Hashtag` looks almost as predictive (η² = 0.75) and the field would
naturally rank hashtags and recommend the winners. It is a confound: hashtags are unevenly
distributed across categories, so the hashtag inherits its category's engagement. Controlling for
category, the **within-category η² collapses to 0.018**.
**Choosing a different hashtag inside the same category changes essentially nothing.**

> **Two estimators, and the report now quotes one of them.** The figure above is the *pooled*
> within-category η² - between-cell sums of squares summed over categories, over within-category
> total sums of squares - which is **0.018092**. The n-weighted mean of the five separate
> per-category η² (Educational 0.007, Entertainment 0.002, Customer Story 0.006, Event 0.002,
> Product Promotion 0.076) is **0.0224**, and that is the number this profile originally published
> and the dashboard originally repeated. Neither is wrong, but the dashboard renders the pooled
> form - it is the one that equals the semi-partial (R²cells - R²category)/(1 - R²category), and
> the one `model/metric_checks.yml::mirage_eta2_within_category` recomputes against the parquet.
> The report quotes 0.018 everywhere as of 2025-06-30. See `analysis/insights.md` I-6.

**Timing has no answer either, on either measure.** Views by hour: Kruskal p = 0.85; by day of week
p = 0.34. Engagement rate by hour: p = 0.19; by day of week p = 0.83. Requirement R4 ("ideal days
and hours") has no positive answer, and saying so with the test is more useful than ranking twelve
statistically identical hours. The earlier draft of this section tested only the engagement rate -
the column this profile spends DQ1 disqualifying - so both measures are reported now (`insights.md`
I-7).

## What this means for G3

**Corrected thesis: this file has one real variable.** `Views` is the only quantity that is neither
derived from another column nor drawn from a label, and what moves it is the **format** you publish
(Post_Type, 19.0% of log-view variance, Video 3.4x Image) - not the platform (0.4%), the region
(0.2%, n.s.) or the hour (n.s.). Organic-vs-sponsored *is* significant on views, but only as a
proxy for format: it adds partial η²=0.0049 to a format model and reverses inside Video (I-8).

G3's job:
1. Size the format effect on views properly, with confidence intervals.
2. Work the rate-vs-volume split, which is real *as an accounting fact* even though the rate is a
   label: Product Promotion takes 40.1% of views for 34.5% of engagement, Customer Story 7.6% of
   views for 11.2% of engagement. That is a genuine mix statement about where attention goes.
3. Decide how much of the report should say "this metric is synthetic". Honesty is the
   differentiator, but a poster that only says "nothing here is real" is not a report. The line to
   walk: report what the data CAN support (views by format, structural availability of clicks and
   video), and flag what it cannot.
4. Keep the hashtag-mirage result - it survives adversarial review intact and is the single
   strongest finding: hashtag adds +0.0037 R^2 once category is known; category adds +0.0468 once
   hashtag is known.

Five of the brief's nine questions will be answered in the negative with evidence. On this data that
is the correct answer, and no published entry gives it.
