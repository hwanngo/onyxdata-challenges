## Top 5 data quality issues shaping this analysis

The five that changed what this analysis could say, in the order they bind:

1. **`is_fraud_cluster` is an unadjudicated vendor label flagging 50.2% of users.** No real
   abuse population has that prevalence. Inheriting the word would have told leadership that
   half their paying subscribers are criminals. Everything downstream is named for the
   behaviour actually measured - repetition - and the column name is disclosed once per
   surface.
2. **`variety = distinct ÷ sessions` is mechanically bounded by 774/n**, and the cohort has
   2.3× more sessions (Spearman between sessions and the ratio: -0.705). Part of the raw
   d = -0.92 was arithmetic. Rarefaction to a fixed 30 plays is the fix, and the effect gets
   *stronger* (-0.9739), which is the outcome that licenses the claim. The within-user
   Herfindahl has the same bounded shape and is quoted un-rarefied - flagged in
   `assumptions.md`.
3. **`playlist_id` is random with respect to the track played** - 3.649% coherence against
   3.680% expected by chance, z = -0.76. A playlist join would have meant nothing, so the
   model demotes it to one boolean and raises if coherence ever doubles.
4. **`new_artist_discovered` has 28% recall** against true first-plays (17,474 flags against
   50,351 real first-plays, 82.36% row agreement). Any "discovery rate" chart would have been
   a chart of the flag, not of discovery.
5. **`estimated_revenue_usd` is 0.508% of the business and mixes ad income with royalty
   cost** - on Free it is income, on paid tiers it is money paid out. Summing it produces a
   number that is neither. There is no column named `revenue` in the curated model.

## 1. The dictionary is true, which changes what this month is

This is the first month in the programme where the shipped documentation is trustworthy. It means
the deliverable is an analysis, not an audit.

| Claim | Verdict |

| Free revenue = `(listen_seconds/60)*0.003` | True, then rounded to 4 dp. 32,614 rows differ by ≤ $0.0001; **+$0.87 on $852.14**. |

## 4. Two confounds that had to be removed before anything could be claimed

- **`variety` is bounded by 774/n.** Only 774 tracks exist, so distinct/sessions falls
  mechanically as sessions rise (ρ = -0.705), and flagged users have 2.3× more sessions. The raw
  d = -0.919 was therefore partly arithmetic. **Rarefaction** - sample exactly *k* plays per user -
  removes it, and the effect grows: d = -0.9739 at k=30, -0.9875 at k=60, -0.9879 at k=120.
- **Month-of-year is bounded by the growth ramp.** Session volume rises 13 → 14,795 across 48
  months, so *any* within-year slice makes later months look bigger. Detrending (log-linear fit,
  test month-of-year on residuals) removes the entire apparent season. η² is unusable here - 4
  observations per group - so **ω²** is reported, and it is negative.

## 5. The headline number is a category error

`estimated_revenue_usd` totals **$852.14** over 224,078 sessions and 48 months. The same users'
subscriptions generate **$167,647.19** - **197×** more. The column is 0.508% of the business.

It is also not one quantity: on Free it is ad **income**; on Premium/Family the dictionary calls it
*"per-stream royalty"*, which is money the platform **pays**. Summing the column adds a cost to a
revenue.

The royalty side is also unstable by construction: it qualifies at **≥30 seconds** and **46.9% of
all sessions land in the 30-44s band**. Moving the threshold to 32s cuts qualifying plays 15.1%; to
35s, 38.0%.

## 6. A third of reported growth is not growth

Net MRR change over 48 months is **+$8,143.50** (expansion $13,808.96, contraction -$5,665.46).
But `trigger_context = 'reconciliation'` - which appears **only** on upgrades (440 of 1,059) and
downgrades (215 of 336), never on signup, churn or retention - accounts for **$2,648.24, or 32.5%**
of the net. Customer-driven net MRR is **$5,495.26**, and reconciliation's share rises every year:
$164.87 → $564.63 → $669.56 → $1,249.18.

## 7. Grain, restated

`fact_listening_session` is one row per play: `session_id` unique across 224,078 rows. But it is
**not** one row per user-track-time: 3.649% playlist coherence means `playlist_id` should be
dropped from any grouping. The honest grain for analysis is
**user × track × device × timestamp**, with `playlist_id` carrying one usable bit (public/private).
